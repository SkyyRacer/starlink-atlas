 BACKGROUND:
  The Advanced Composition Explorer (ACE) collects and analyzes particles of 
  solar, interplanetary, interstellar, and galactic origins. The data advances
  understanding of the Sun, its interaction with Earth, and the evolution of 
  the solar system. 

  ACE additionally provides real-time space weather data and advanced warning
  of geomagnetic storms. 

  ACE was launched 25 August 1997 @ 14:39 UTC. Four hours after launch, the 
  spacecraft's Delta 2 launch vehicle second stage re-ignited to insert the 
  satellite into a 177 x 1.37 million-kilometer orbit.

  Upon reaching apogee a month after launch, ACE was maneuvered to a Lissajous 
  orbit around the Earth-Sun Lagrange 1 point, arriving on December 12, 1997. 
  L1 lies between the Sun and the Earth at a distance of 1,500,000 km from 
  Earth, avoiding effects from the Earth's magnetic field. The mission was 
  declared operational on 1998-Jan-21.

  The spacecraft continues operating from that orbit with enough fuel remaining
  to potentially support operations until 2024, having functioned significantly 
  beyond its expected run-time.
 
 SPACECRAFT PHYSICAL CHARACTERISTICS:
  Total launch mass: 785 kg (incl. 189 kg of hydrazine fuel)
  Dimensions       : 1.6 x 1 m (excluding 4 solar arrays and magnetometer boom)
  Solar arrays     : 500 Watts
  Spin             : 5 rpm, spin axis ~ Earth-Sun line, instruments sunward 
  Communications   : S-band, 78 & 6.9 kbps, and 434 bps 
  Storage          : 2 gigabits solid-state on-board
  Launch vehicle   : Delta II 7920-8 (no. D247)

 SCIENCE INSTRUMENTS
  The spacecraft carries six high-resolution sensors and three monitoring 
  instruments that sample low-energy particles of solar origin and high-energy
  galactic particles:

  * Solar Wind Ion Mass Spectrometer (SWIMS) and Solar Wind Ion Composition
     Spectrometer (SWICS)
  * Ultra-Low Energy Isotope Spectrometer (ULEIS)
  * Solar Energetic Particle Ionic Charge Analyzer (SEPICA)
     (no data return since Feb 4, 2005)
  * Solar Isotope Spectrometer (SIS)
  * Cosmic Ray Isotope Spectrometer (CRIS)
  * Solar Wind Electron, Proton, and Alpha Monitor (SWEPAM)
  * Electron, Proton, and Alpha-Particle Monitor (EPAM)
  * Magnetometer (MAG)
  * Real Time Solar Wind Experiment (RTSW)
